1. Planetary Health Diet Linked to 29% Lower Dementia Risk and 2.17-Year Delay in Onset — Brain Autopsy Data Included

Source: Alzheimer's & Dementia — Association of the Planetary Health Diet With Dementia Risk and Brain Pathology (2026)

What happened: A study published in Alzheimer's & Dementia followed 926 dementia-free adults with a mean age of 79.4 years in the Rush Memory and Aging Project for up to 20 years. High adherence to the Planetary Health Diet (PHD) — the EAT-Lancet Commission's evidence-based dietary framework emphasising plant foods and minimal animal products — was associated with a 29% lower risk of all-cause dementia (HR = 0.71) and a 30% lower risk of Alzheimer's disease specifically (HR = 0.70). Among 581 decedents who underwent brain autopsy, high PHD adherence was associated with meaningfully reduced Alzheimer's pathology markers. Onset of dementia was delayed by an average of 2.17 years in high adherers. This is among the first studies to directly link a sustainable dietary pattern to both clinical dementia outcomes and neuropathological markers in very old adults.

Why this matters to you: This study was conducted in a population with a mean age squarely within your target demographic — adults approaching and beyond 80 — and used brain autopsy data to validate dietary associations with actual neuropathology, not just cognitive test performance. The PHD is also an environmentally sustainable diet, giving you a dual frame (personal health + planetary health) that may be particularly motivating for values-aligned older adults who want their food choices to matter beyond their own wellbeing.

Second-order effect: No prior study had examined PHD adherence in relation to neuropathology in living-to-autopsy cohort data. The finding that this dietary pattern reduces objectively measured Alzheimer's pathology — not just clinical diagnosis — substantially strengthens the mechanistic case for dietary intervention and positions this as potentially landmark evidence for the field.

Why this matters to you: The 74% figure confirms that your 75+ focus is precisely targeted at the demographic bearing the greatest dementia burden.
